## Generate recovery words

Use the device on battery power, with both USB cables disconnected. Open
**Tools → Generate a seed**, then choose the camera or dice.

### Camera

Point the camera at a varied scene, capture a picture, and inspect it. Retake
it or choose **Use picture**, then choose 12 or 24 words. The device combines
preview frames, the accepted capture and fresh hardware randomness. It does not
save the picture. Back up the generated words: taking the same picture again
does not recover the same identity.

### Dice

Use a physical six-sided die and independent rolls. Choose 12 words for 50 rolls
or 24 words for 100 rolls. Enter each result in order; correct mistakes before
generating the seed. Do not invent a sequence. The device cannot measure whether
your die or rolls are random.

The exact digit sequence is hashed with SHA-256. The first 16 bytes produce a
12-word seed; all 32 bytes produce a 24-word seed. No device randomness is mixed
into this method. Keep the recovery words as your backup.

## Choose a backup

Recovery words restore a derived identity. An nsec restores one private key.
They are different formats; keep the format and any account choice with your backup.

Write words in order or use the device's QR backup. Keep secrets away from cloud
storage and connected computers or cameras. Use only an offline receiver when
you choose QR backup. Anyone who obtains a full private-key backup may use your identity.

## Keep the passphrase

A BIP39 passphrase changes the derived key. It is not a password that can be reset.
You need the same words, passphrase and account to recover the same identity.

Check the resulting npub. A different passphrase can produce a valid but different
identity, without an error message.

### Enter a passphrase

From **Identity ready**, choose **Add / change passphrase**. For a loaded identity,
open **Key options** first. Choose **Type passphrase** or **Scan passphrase**.

For scanning, prepare a QR containing only the passphrase as plain UTF-8 text on
an offline device. The limit is **256 UTF-8 bytes**; some characters use several
bytes. Never create or scan this secret QR through the companion, a website or
a connected computer.

Read every page of **Check passphrase**, including spaces and escaped characters.
Choose **Use passphrase** to apply it, then check the full npub. Cancelling keeps
the loaded identity.

## Check recovery

Choose **Check my backup** to answer word-position questions. For a QR backup,
choose **Scan to check**. These checks compare your backup without clearing the
loaded identity. A seed QR contains the words, without the passphrase or account.

### Copy a SeedQR to paper

For 12 or 24 words, choose **SeedQR** or **Compact SeedQR** from the backup menu.
Choose **Copy** and copy each enlarged block at its shown coordinates.
Keep the outer white border clear. Return to the whole QR when needed, then
scan the paper copy with **Scan to check**. Finish only after it matches.

For 15, 18 or 21 words, back up the words and use the word-position check.
SeedQR and Compact SeedQR are not offered for these lengths.

## Restore an identity

Choose **Load a key**, then scan a key QR or enter words or an nsec. For words, choose
the word count and type a short prefix. Tap the matching word. Go back to correct
a word; a checksum error keeps your entries available for editing.

Add the same passphrase and account, if used. Compare the complete npub with your
recorded identity. Never enter recovery words into the companion or a client.

Store separate copies in places you can recover. Do not keep the only backup
inside the device or beside the only copy of a passphrase.

## Back up a group share

A [2-of-3 group](/docs/threshold/) has three different shares. Back up each one.
Several copies of the same member's share still count as one member. Keep the
shares separate: two distinct shares can sign for the group.

On **Group ready**, choose **Back up this share**. For an already loaded group,
choose **Back up key**. The share backup is a distinct format; it is not
recovery words or an nsec.

### Save to an SD card

Choose **Save to SD card**, read the confirmation, then choose **Save share**.
The device writes an **unencrypted** `NSEALR.NSR` file at the root of a FAT-formatted
card. Keep the card offline and separate from other members' backups.

The device never formats a card or replaces an existing file. If `NSEALR.NSR`
already exists, use a different card. Wait for **Share saved**: the device writes
the file and reads it back before showing success. An error means the save was
not verified; keep the loaded share while you resolve it.

### Save an animated QR

Choose **Show offline QR → Show share**. Capture every frame on an offline
receiver. Use **Pause**, **Next** and **Resume** as needed; one photograph may
contain only part of the backup. Never scan this private QR into the companion.

Choose **Done**, then **Scan to check**. Present every saved frame back to the
device. **Backup checked** means it matches the loaded share. This comparison
does not replace the loaded share.

## Restore a group share

Choose **Tools → Restore a group share**, then:

- **Scan share QR**: show every frame from the offline backup.
- **Read from SD card**: insert the card containing `NSEALR.NSR` at its root.

Review the group fingerprint, member and Nostr identity against your separately
kept public group record before approving. A restored share belongs to the same
member; restore a different member's share on the other signing device.

Keep the backups until you have checked the restored identity. Restoring a copy
does not revoke an old or stolen copy of that share.

If a member and its backup are lost, use both remaining devices to
[replace the lost member](/docs/threshold/#replace-a-lost-member). This preserves
the npub and requires new backups after repair. It does not revoke old copies.
